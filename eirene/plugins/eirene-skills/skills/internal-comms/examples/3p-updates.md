# 3P updates

## What it is

A 3P update covers **Progress, Plans, Problems** for a team over a period,
almost always one week. Readers are executives, leadership and other teams:
people with some context on the team but not much. It should take 30–60 seconds
to read.

A 3P can describe any size of group, up to a whole company. The bigger the group,
the coarser the items: a mobile team might report "shipped the offline mode" or
"fixed the login crash"; a company might report "hired 20 people" or "closed 10
deals".

- **Progress** — what was done in the period: things shipped, milestones reached,
  work started.
- **Plans** — what the team will do next period: the top priorities.
- **Problems** — what is slowing the team down: too few people, blocking bugs, a
  deal that fell through, a dependency that slipped.

Know the team name before writing. If it was not given, ask.

## Sources

Pull from what you can reach:

- team chat: update posts from team members, especially in busy channels or with
  many reactions;
- documents written by key people on the team that many people viewed;
- email threads with many replies or substantial content;
- calendar: one-off meetings that mattered, such as product reviews.

Match each section to its window:

- Progress: from a week ago to today
- Plans: from today to a week from now
- Problems: from a week ago to today

Without access to sources, ask the person what to cover. They may give you the
items directly, in which case your job is mostly formatting.

## Workflow

1. **Scope:** confirm the team name and the dates (usually the past week for
   Progress and Problems, the coming week for Plans).
2. **Gather:** use available sources or ask.
3. **Draft:** use the exact format below.
4. **Review:** readable in 30–60 seconds, factual, with numbers where they exist.

## Format

The format is fixed. Use nothing else. Choose one emoji that fits the team and the
mood of the week.

```
[emoji] [Team name] ([dates covered])
Progress: [1–3 sentences]
Plans: [1–3 sentences]
Problems: [1–3 sentences]
```

Each section is one to three clear sentences. Be concrete and include metrics
where possible. The tone is matter-of-fact, not prose.
